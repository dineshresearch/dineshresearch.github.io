Compute budget

The full 130-run matrix was measured at 3.5 h (207 min) of scored-run wall time (reactive 13.5 min, planner 18.4 min, BC 6.8 min, Inkling-2 61 min, MiniMax-M2.7 108 min), plus bounded retry and infrastructure-recovery overhead; elapsed evaluation time was ~5 h including provider-timeout recovery passes. Inkling-2 incurred no per-token charges (rate-limited serving); the MiniMax-M2.7 sweep ran under explicit budget authorization with provider cost reporting absent. GPU-trained RL specialists are explicitly outside scope and deferred.

Under the strict loop each 0.1 s of simulated flight costs ~4 generations (think–observe–think–step), so 100 turns purchase only ~23–30 environment steps. The traces show the models piloting correctly during those seconds: every executed step uses the maximum legal 10 ticks, commanded velocities are stable at 1.0–1.2 m/s along the corridor, and all 18 episodes record zero collision events (minimum clearance 0.21 m). Episodes end at budget with the drone ~33 m from the goal: throughput starvation, not a control failure. The frozen opencode harness batches tool calls within a turn, which is why the frozen arms reach the detour and exhibit the capability boundary while the extension arms are throughput-bound before reaching it. The two regimes must not be conflated, and model scale moves neither barrier.

03Commander micro-experiment (development probe)

Can a hosted open-weight model serve the commander role: decompose a natural-language mission into ordered, legal subgoals over a fixed skill menu (go_to, rotate, hold, inspect, search, finished), emit pilot questions, constraints and an escalation policy, and respect bounds and checkpoint order? Four missions: M1_pad, M2_inspect_sequence, M3_search_hidden, M4_infeasible (waypoint outside legal bounds). Single attempt each; heuristic structural checks, not mission execution.

Every policy clears the first checkpoint (straight corridor transit); only the mapping planner consistently progresses beyond it. The first detour is the capability boundary already in the pilot.

Vision-language pilot. On the same bridge layout with RGB+depth, Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL (full budgets: 100 turns, 1800 s) grounded the scene correctly — identified the barrier from RGB, cross-checked its extent against depth, committed to a right-side detour — then misjudged near-edge clearance and contacted the barrier corner at t=7.25 s (position ≈(6.25, 0.93, 1.38) m; 1/5 checkpoints, 10%; 53 turns, 509 s wall). Perception and intent correct; geometric clearance reasoning not. Other endpoints tested (GLM-5.3-Flash, MiniMax-M2.7) reject image parts at the provider (HTTP 400), so vision capability and model identity are confounded in this pilot and separated in the frozen protocol’s sensor tracks.

Failure modes. (i) Contact at the detour edge (BC, both LLMs, vision model): lateral commitment without sufficient clearance margin. (ii) Schema violation: Inkling-2 issued a 25-tick hold against the documented 1–10 limit (invalid_action); the environment was not relaxed. (iii) Infrastructure: MiniMax-M2.7 intermittently ended its first turn as text-only with zero tool calls; 2 of 4 pilot runs failed this way, one reproducibly after the bounded retry. Such runs are recorded with null metrics, never imputed.

Learned-policy negative results. The first BC fit imitated all four command components including yaw and collapsed in closed loop (contact at 4–18 s on every world) despite low validation MSE: the planner’s sensing-scan yaw is not identifiable from the current observation, and the imitated yaw bias rotated the depth cone off the travel direction. The primary BC comparator therefore imitates translational velocity only (3 outputs) and shares the planner’s target-bearing yaw rule at serve time. One DAgger iteration (40 BC-executed, planner-labelled rollouts) improved validation MSE (0.0279→0.0135) but worsened closed-loop behavior (0/5 checkpoints, contact ~27 s): the bottleneck is missing episode memory, not covariate shift. BC tuning stopped after these two iterations; no baseline was tuned toward a desired score.

The architecture these measurements support is a language model at the slow semantic layers (commander, advisory) above a fast classical or memory-equipped learned inner loop. The double dissociation — the same model commands well and pilots badly — is the evidence that the two roles should be separated rather than merged. Scoped to the tested models under one frozen protocol with a paused clock, five seeds per world, and a behavior-cloned rather than RL-tuned learned comparator; the frontier extension finds that hosted frontier models do not move the boundary under the strict-loop interface.
